# Local SEO (`/BWESEOLocal`)

Apply only when `markets.locationDependence` is `local`, `regional` or `multi-location`.
For `non-local` businesses, skip location pages entirely.

## Checks

- **NAP**: name, address, phone identical across site, schema, Google Business Profile (ask the
  user for GBP status — the engine can't see it), and major directories.
- **Address type**: storefront/office (public) vs. service-area business (hide street address in
  GBP, use `areaServed`) vs. mailing address/PO box (not a location — never present as an office).
- **Primary location page / home page** clearly states where the business is and whom it serves.
- **Service areas** described honestly: which towns, how service works there (on-site, remote,
  travel limits, fees).
- **Reviews**: how the business asks for them (GBP link), never gated or incentivized; never
  copied onto the site as fake testimonials.

## Location pages — rules

- Only for places the business genuinely serves. One per legitimate market, not one per town.
- Each needs **unique value**: local specifics that matter to the customer (travel/visit policy,
  local examples with permission, relevant local regulations/climate/industry mix, directions if a
  storefront, local proof). If the only difference is the city name → doorway page → reject.
- Never claim an office, staff or presence that doesn't exist. Say plainly when work is remote.
- No lists of dozens of towns stuffed into copy; a short "areas we serve" list is fine.

## Output

Local findings (appended to baseline), recommended GBP actions for the owner (the engine does not
log into GBP), and which location pages to keep / improve / merge / retire.
